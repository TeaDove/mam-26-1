Research on controlled rolling was carried out by the BISRA group in the 1960s, which opened the way for the development of the present controlled-rolling technology. It is difficult to pinpoint the beginning, but the development of C—Mn (manganese)—Nb (niobium) fine-grained steel commenced about 1958. Duckworth et al. showed that with the C content below 0.1%, and by using Nb additions and a low finishing temperature with heavy draughting, very attractive properties could be obtained in the as-rolled condition. Pearlite-free steel and pearlite-reduced steel proposed by Duckworth et al. were intended to improve weldability and toughness by reducing the C content, to increase strength by additions of Nb and/or V, and to improve toughness by using a low finishing temperature. It was Phillips and Chapman, Irani et al. and Jones and Rothwell who, for the first time, turned their attention to controlled rolling itself. They showed that Nb retarded recrystallization greatly, thereby enabling the deformation of γ (austenite) in the non-recrystallization region, that transformation from deformed γ produced a fine α-grain (ferrite grain) structure, and that an improper draught schedule tended to cause a mixed grain structure. However, because their experiments were carried out in a laboratory bar mill, and the only measure of controlled rolling was the finishing temperature, their results lacked generality.

Research on controlled rolling motivated by the BISRA group has been undertaken very actively, including fundamental and applied aspects. Fundamental aspects include items such as the recrystallization behaviour of γ the retardation mechanism of recrystallization due to Nb, microstructural change accompanying deformation in the non-recrystallization γ-region and its influence on transformation behaviour, and factors governing grain structure after transformation.

Through these investigations, the importance of deformation in the non-recrystallization region was recognized. It was also made clear that controlled rolling was not to cease in the γ-region, but was to be extended into the γ–α-region (austenite–ferrite region), thereby enhancing both strength and toughness. Modern controlled rolling means the overall technology consisting of (i) a suitable selection of chemical composition, (ii) a proper slab-reheating temperature to obtain small and uniform γ-grains, (iii) γ-grain refinement owing to repeated deformation recrystallization, (iv) deformation in the non-recrystallization region, (v) deformation in the γ–α two-phase region, and (vi) controlled cooling.

The full-scale production of controlled-rolled plates and hot-rolled strip commenced with the construction of large-diameter pipelines, which required high-strength steel with good toughness and weldability. In fact, pipelines applied to Arctic-temperature service were impossible to construct without controlled-rolled plates.

Finally, the great contribution from Hall and Petch must be recognized. The Hall-Petch relation correlates yield stress with ferrite grain size. Heslop and Petch also associated transition temperature with grain size; they guided the development of controlled rolling throughout its whole course.
