Sekine and Maruyama showed that α-grain structure is decided by the recrystallized γ-grain size before deformation in the non-recrystallization region and the amount of deformation in that region. Figure 3 depicts the effect of total reduction in the non-recrystallization region on α-grain size (Fig. 3a) and upper bainite volume fraction (Fig. 3b). When a recrystallized γ-grain (austenite grain) size is large, it is difficult to suppress the formation of upper bainite even by large deformation. However, when the recrystallized grain size is small, upper bainite is not allowed to form, even with moderate deformation, giving fine-grained α (ferrite). Sekine and Maruyama emphasized that the best way is to refine γ through recrystallization as much as possible (type 1B), followed by deformation in the non-recrystallization region (type 2) and whereby a uniform, fine-grained α-structure is produced.

The difference between Fukuda et al. and Sekine and Maruyama is that the former stressed type 2 to achieve a fine-grained α-structure, whereas the latter emphasized the importance of the combination of type 1B and 2. This argument is, however, reduced to the uniformity of α nucleation within unrecrystallized γ-grains which, in turn, is reduced to the uniformity in deformation-band density throughout unrecrystallized grain, since α nucleates on deformation bands as well as at γ-grain boundary. There has been some evidence that deformation-band density has the propensity to be heterogeneous and that not all the deformation band have the same ferrite-nucleating potential, suggesting that type 2 tends to produce a mixed α-grain structure compared with the optimum combination of type 1B and 2.

So far, controlled rolling has been confined to deformation in the γ-region. Coleman et al. and Gladman and Dulieu obtained a further increase in strength by extending deformation into the γ + α (austenite + ferrite) and/or α-region, and by developing a substructure. Advancing this idea, Tanaka et al. proposed the three stage of the controlled-rolling process: (i) deformation in the γ-recrystallization region, (ii) deformation in the non-recrystallization region, and (iii) deformation in the γ–α (austenite–ferrite) two-phase region.

Figure 4 illustrates schematically the three stage of the controlled-rolling process, and the microstructural change accompanying deformation in each stage. Stage 1: coarse austenite $a$ is refined by repeated deformation and recrystallization $b$, but still transforms to relatively coarse ferrite $b'$; stage 2: deformation band are formed in elongated, unrecrystallized austenite $c$, and ferrite nucleates on the deformation bands as well as γ-grain boundary, giving fine α $c'$; stage 3: deformation in the γ–α two-phase region continues stage 2 and also deforms the ferrite, producing a substructure $d$. During cooling after deformation, unrecrystallized austenite transforms to equiaxed α-grain, while deformed ferrite changes into subgrain $d'$.

Grain refinement through repeated recrystallization does not proceed indefinitely but reaches a certain limiting value $b$ and thereby results in relatively coarse α-grain $b'$. A means of breaking the limiting value is to divide the γ-grain. Since a recrystallized γ-grain (austenite grain) $b$ is divided by deformation band $c$, the final α-grain structure is much smaller for $c'$ than for $b'$. The deformation in the two-phase region causes further grain refinement and a mixed structure consisting of equiaxed grain and subgrain. Additional strengthening is obtained by subgrain hardening.
