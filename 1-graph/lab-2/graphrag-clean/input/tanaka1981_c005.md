The difference between Fukuda et al. and Sekine and Maruyama is that the former stressed type 2 to achieve a fine-grained α-structure (ferrite structure), whereas the latter emphasized the importance of the combination of types 1B and 2. This argument is, however, reduced to the uniformity of α nucleation within unrecrystallized γ-grains (austenite grains) which, in turn, is reduced to the uniformity in deformation-band density throughout unrecrystallized grains, since α nucleates on deformation bands as well as at γ-grain boundaries. There has been some evidence that deformation-band density has the propensity to be heterogeneous and that not all the deformation bands have the same ferrite-nucleating potential, suggesting that type 2 tends to produce a mixed α-grain structure compared with the optimum combination of types 1B and 2.

So far, controlled rolling has been confined to deformation in the γ-region. Coleman et al. and Gladman and Dulieu obtained a further increase in strength by extending deformation into the γ + α (austenite + ferrite) and/or α-regions, and by developing a substructure. Advancing this idea, Tanaka et al. proposed the three stages of the controlled-rolling process: (i) deformation in the γ-recrystallization region, (ii) deformation in the non-recrystallization region, and (iii) deformation in the γ–α (austenite–ferrite) two-phase region.

Figure 4 illustrates schematically the three stages of the controlled-rolling process, and the microstructural change accompanying deformation in each stage. Stage 1: coarse austenite $a$ is refined by repeated deformation and recrystallization $b$, but still transforms to relatively coarse ferrite $b'$; stage 2: deformation bands are formed in elongated, unrecrystallized austenite $c$, and ferrite nucleates on the deformation bands as well as γ-grain boundaries, giving fine α $c'$; stage 3: deformation in the γ–α two-phase region continues stage 2 and also deforms the ferrite, producing a substructure $d$. During cooling after deformation, unrecrystallized austenite transforms to equiaxed α-grains, while deformed ferrite changes into subgrains $d'$.

Grain refinement through repeated recrystallization does not proceed indefinitely but reaches a certain limiting value $b$ and thereby results in relatively coarse α-grains $b'$. A means of breaking the limiting value is to divide the γ-grain. Since a recrystallized γ-grain $b$ is divided by deformation bands $c$, the final α-grain structure is much smaller for $c'$ than for $b'$. The deformation in the two-phase region causes further grain refinement and a mixed structure consisting of equiaxed grains and subgrains. Additional strengthening is obtained by subgrain hardening.
