The fundamental difference between conventional hot rolling and controlled rolling lies in that, in the former, α (ferrite) nucleates exclusively at γ-grain boundary, whereas in the latter α nucleation occurs at grain interiors as well as grain boundary, which leads to a large difference in the final α-grain structure between the two. The fact that the deformation band is equivalent to the γ-grain boundary with regard to ferrite-nucleating potentiality, means that a γ-grain is divided into several blocks by deformation band. A comparison between conventional hot rolling and controlled rolling is very similar to that between normalizing and quenching. In conventionally hot-rolled and normalized steel, α nucleates exclusively at γ-grain boundary, which limits grain refinement, whereas in controlled-rolled and quenched steel, the γ-grain is divided into several blocks; the former is by deformation band and the latter by martensitic transformation, producing a much finer grain structure.

Since unrecrystallized γ containing deformation band promotes the γ to α transformation, from a different viewpoint, controlled rolling can be defined as a process for accelerating α transformation and thereby for producing fine-grained α in steel with medium hardenability which, without being controlled rolled, will transform to a bainitic structure, giving poor toughness.
