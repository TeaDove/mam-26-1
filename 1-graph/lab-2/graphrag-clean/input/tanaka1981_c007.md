## DEFORMATION IN RECRYSTALLIZATION REGION

## Dynamic recrystallization

There are two kinds of restoration process associated with hot working, dynamic and static. When steel is deformed in the austenitic state at high temperatures, the flow stress rises to a maximum and then falls to a steady state, see Fig. 5a. In a range of strain less than that for the peak stress dynamic recovery operates, while in the steady-state region dynamic recrystallization occurs. The structures developed by dynamic restoration are thermodynamically unstable, and when held at temperature they are modified by the static-restoration process. Three static-restoration processes can be operative, static recovery, static recrystallization, and metadynamic (post-dynamic) recrystallization, see Fig. 5b.

The dynamically recrystallized grain size d is associated with the Zener–Hollomon parameter Z, which is a temperature-compensated strain rate, and is given by the following equations:

$$
d ^ {- 1} \propto \sigma_ {\mathrm{flow}} \propto \log Z \tag{1}
$$

$$
Z = \dot {\epsilon} \exp \left(Q _ {\mathrm{dif}} / R T\right) \tag{2}
$$

where $\dot{\epsilon}$ is the strain rate, $Q_{\mathrm{dif}}$ is the activation energy for the self-diffusion of iron, $R$ is the gas constant, and $T$ is absolute temperature. This relation was found to exist for Nb (niobium) steel hot rolled under a certain condition, shown in Fig. 6. However, since a critical strain for the onset of steady state is very large, even at high temperatures, it is almost impossible to hot roll steel, and in particular Nb steel, in the steady-state region. This means, in turn, that it is almost impossible to refine the γ-grain (austenite grain) through dynamic recrystallization. Instead, grain refinement must be achieved by static recrystallization.
