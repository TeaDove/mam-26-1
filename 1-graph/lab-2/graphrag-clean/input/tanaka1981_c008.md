## Static recrystallization

Static recrystallization accompanying hot deformation proceeds in a similar manner to that in cold-worked material. Nucleation sites for new grains are predominantly triple junctions of grains and grain boundaries, and nucleation within grains is almost nil. The distribution of nuclei is highly localized and inhomogeneous; while some grain boundaries give rise to numerous nuclei, there are boundaries where nuclei are completely absent. The progress of recrystallization after nucleation is essentially the migration of the recrystallizing front into deformed matrix as is usual in recrystallization. The completion of recrystallization is followed by normal grain growth.

Effects of the amount of deformation and deformation temperature on recrystallized grain size are shown in Fig.7. The recrystallized grain size decreases rapidly as the amount of reduction increases, reaching a limiting value. The limiting grain size decreases with reducing initial grain size. Although a lower deformation temperature produces smaller grain sizes, the effect of temperature is slight. Since nucleation sites for recrystallization are predominantly at the grain boundaries, the initial grain size plays an important role in determining recrystallized grain size. Ouchi et al. showed that recrystallized grain size decreases in a linear manner with the product of strain and total grain-boundary area. Using stainless steel, Towle and Gladman showed that recrystallized grain size $d_{rex}$ can be expressed by

$$
d _ {\mathrm{rex}} = k \epsilon^ {- 0.5} d _ {0} Z ^ {- 0.06} \quad , \tag{3}
$$

where $k$ is a constant, $\epsilon$ is the amount of strain, $d_0$ is the initial grain size, and $Z$ is the Zener-Hollomon parameter. Equation (3) also indicates the slight effect of strain rate on recrystallized grain size.
