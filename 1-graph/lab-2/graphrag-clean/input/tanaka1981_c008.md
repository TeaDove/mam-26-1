## Conditions for recrystallization

Taking static recrystallization after hot deformation as a prerequisite condition, the effect of various factor on recrystallized grain size have been described so far. It must be stressed, however, that static recrystallization accompanying hot deformation does not occur under all condition.

On the contrary, it is necessary to apply more deformation than that required for recrystallization. The critical amount of deformation, $R_{crit}$ , required for the completion of recrystallization is small, as is the temperature dependence in plain C steel. In Nb steel, however, $R_{crit}$ is very large and its temperature dependence substantially higher. The effect of deformation temperature and initial grain size on $R_{crit}$ are shown in Fig. 9. In Nb steel, the effect of grain size on $R_{crit}$ is extremely large, and $R_{crit}$ increases rapidly with decreasing deformation temperature. Recrystallization is virtually suppressed below 950 °C.

Ouchi et al. and Tanaka et al. studied the general recrystallization behaviour by single-pass rolling, shown in Fig. 10. Recrystallization behaviour is divided into three region, recovery, partial recrystallization, and recrystallization. The recrystallization region is subdivided into static and dynamic. The critical amount of deformation dividing each region increases rapidly with decreasing deformation temperature. It is also influenced markedly by the addition of microalloying elements, and in particular Nb. Reduction in the recrystallization region produces a fine, uniform recrystallized grain structure. Reduction in the partial-recrystallization region produces a mixed structure consisting of recrystallized grain and recovered grain. Reduction in the recovery region produces locally very coarse grain due to strain-induced grain-boundary migration, while most grain remain unchanged, releasing stored energy by recovery, As is clear from Fig. 10, it is very difficult to roll steel in the recrystallization region and, in particular, during the initial pass in the plate mill, since $R_{\text{crit}}$ is extremely large. In practice, rolling pass are carried out in the recovery region or, at most, in the partial-recrystallization region. Tanaka et al. described clearly how to achieve grain refinement and how to obtain a uniform grain structure under these condition. Even if deformation is applied repeatedly in the recovery region, very sluggish and incomplete recrystallization occurs, while huge grain larger than the initial size are produced locally and persist throughout rolling, see Fig. 11, which leads to the formation of a mixed α-grain (ferrite grain) structure. However, if reduction is applied in the partial-recrystallization region, recrystallization increases in volume fraction with an increasing number of pass, reaching complete recrystallization, see Fig. 12. However, once huge grain are formed by a slight reduction in the recovery region, they persist even after many pass in the partial-recrystallization region. In summarizing the above description, if the initial rolling pass are made in the recovery region, huge grain are formed locally, owing to the strain-induced grain-boundary migration, which persist throughout the whole course of rolling. They transform to upper bainite and/or coarse ferrite grain, giving poor toughness. On the other hand, if a reduction per pass greater than that for partial recrystallization is given successively, a uniform and refined grain structure is obtained. Once complete recrystallization takes place, successive recrystallization proceeds much more easily.
