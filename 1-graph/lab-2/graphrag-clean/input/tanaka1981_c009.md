## Formation of mixed grain structure

Controlled rolling produces a finer α-grain size than not only conventional hot rolling but normalizing. Unfortunately, controlled-rolled steel often exhibits a mixed structure consisting of fine and coarse α-grain and/or upper bainite, decreasing low-temperature toughness.

Mixed grain structure of α is attributed to that of γ (austenite) . Under the circumstances where partial recrystallization occurs, fine recrystallized grain tend to form at γ-grain boundary, leaving an unrecrystallized region in the grain interior where the mixed γ-grain structure is produced. When γ-grain are coarse, or when rapid grain growth takes place after recrystallization owing to an unsuitable deformation condition, a mixed γ-grain structure tends to be induced. Once a mixed γ-grain structure occurs, it cannot be eliminated by later rolling pass. This is considered due to the fact that, when rolled again, the recrystallized region in the vicinity of grain boundaries in the previous pass tends to recrystallize preferentially, since it has a smaller grain size than the unrecrystallized region of the grain interior (see Fig. 9). To prevent the formation of a mixed grain structure, suitable condition of holding or a 'continuous' schedule involving reduction in the whole temperature range have been proposed (see Fig. 1). The formation and prevention of a mixed grain structure at high temperature have already been described in the previous section.
