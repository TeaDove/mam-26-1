## DEFORMATION IN NON-RECRYSTALLIZATION REGION

## Retardation of recrystallization

In order to decrease grain size further, deformation in the non-recrystallization region must be applied following deformation in the recrystallization region.

13 Recrystallization of austenite following single deformation of 50% in various steel; after Irvine et al.

In plain C steel, recrystallization becomes sluggish at low temperature in the γ-region (about 900 °C ≈ Ar3 ). Nb has a powerful influence in retarding γ-recrystallization, thereby achieving a non-recrystallized state, see Figs. 13 and 14. They show a marked temperature dependence of the progress of recrystallization, and also a large recrystallization-retarding effect of Nb. Kozasu et al. reported that, besides a very large retarding effect of Nb, V had some retarding effect, while Al (aluminium) and Al+N accelerated recrystallization slightly. Although both V and Nb are strong carbide-forming elements the former does not have any retarding effect, while the latter exhibits a very strong retarding effect. This is considered due to difference in solubility in γ , and strain-induced precipitation potential between the two elements. The retardation of recrystallization due to Nb (niobium) can be exhibited only when Nb is in solution in γ before deformation. If Nb remains undissolved because of a low heating temperature, it does not demonstrate any delaying effect.

14 Fraction recrystallized in austenite as function of holding time in plain C and Nb steel; steel were reheated to 1250 °C, hot rolled to 30%, and held at rolling temperature; after Ohmori

The recrystallization retardation due to Nb has been discussed by many investigators. It has been attributed to (i) a solute-drag effect, (ii) strain-induced precipitation of fine Nb(C,N), or (iii) the combined effect of solute Nb and precipitation of fine Nb(C,N). Coladas et al. reported that the main influence of Nb alloying is on the incubation time for the start of recrystallization, and not on the kinetics of recrystallization after incubation and, further, that the delayed recrystallization is due to a solute effect, see Fig. 15.

The retarding effect due to the strain-induced precipitation of fine Nb(C, N) has been proposed by many investigators using several different approaches. Ohmori suggested a retarding effect due to fine precipitation, based on the fact that the relation between time for recrystallization and the reciprocal of temperature does not obey a linear relation, which is well explained in term of the concurrent precipitation of Nb(C, N). Jones and Rothwell and others observed the precipitation of Nb(C, N) on the deformation substructure of γ (austenite), which retarded subsequent recrystallization. By holding isothermally after hot deformation, Watanabe et al. observed the progress of the precipitation of Nb(C, N), which was determined by the electrolytic-extraction technique, see Fig. 16. The start of precipitation is accelerated markedly by deformation, suggesting interaction between the precipitates and retardation of recrystallization.

Jonas and Weiss reported that the addition of Nb to γ retards recrystallization in two distinct ways; that there is a significant delay introduced by a solute effect, and that under condition where precipitation is more rapid than solute-retarded recrystallization, operation of the recrystallization process is prevented or retarded until precipitation is complete or nearly complete. Combining their result with that of le Bon et al., Jonas and Weiss constructed a recrystallization—precipitation—time (RPT) diagram, shown in Fig. 17. When the time for the start of precipitation $P_{S}$ is longer than the recrystallization start time $R_{S}$, the RPT (recrystallization–precipitation–time) curve has the normal dependence on temperature affected by a solute drag. When $P_{S}$ is less than $R_{S}$, the nucleation and growth of recrystallization is severely impeded.
