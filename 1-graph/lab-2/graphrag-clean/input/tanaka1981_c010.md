Ouchi et al. and Tanaka et al. studied the general recrystallization behaviour by single-pass rolling, shown in Fig. 10. Recrystallization behaviour is divided into three regions, recovery, partial recrystallization, and recrystallization. The recrystallization region is subdivided into static and dynamic. The critical amount of deformation dividing each region increases rapidly with decreasing deformation temperature. It is also influenced markedly by the addition of microalloying elements, and in particular Nb (niobium). Reduction in the recrystallization region produces a fine, uniform recrystallized grain structure. Reduction in the partial-recrystallization region produces a mixed structure consisting of recrystallized grains and recovered grains. Reduction in the recovery region produces locally very coarse grains due to strain-induced grain-boundary migration, while most grains remain unchanged, releasing stored energy by recovery, As is clear from Fig. 10, it is very difficult to roll steel in the recrystallization region and, in particular, during the initial passes in the plate mill, since $R_{\text{crit}}$ is extremely large. In practice, rolling passes are carried out in the recovery region or, at most, in the partial-recrystallization region. Tanaka et al. described clearly how to achieve grain refinement and how to obtain a uniform grain structure under these conditions. Even if deformation is applied repeatedly in the recovery region, very sluggish and incomplete recrystallization occurs, while huge grains larger than the initial size are produced locally and persist throughout rolling, see Fig. 11, which leads to the formation of a mixed α-grain (ferrite grain) structure. However, if reduction is applied in the partial-recrystallization region, recrystallization increases in volume fraction with an increasing number of passes, reaching complete recrystallization, see Fig. 12. However, once huge grains are formed by a slight reduction in the recovery region, they persist even after many passes in the partial-recrystallization region. In summarizing the above description, if the initial rolling passes are made in the recovery region, huge grains are formed locally, owing to the strain-induced grain-boundary migration, which persist throughout the whole course of rolling. They transform to upper bainite and/or coarse ferrite grains, giving poor toughness. On the other hand, if a reduction per pass greater than that for partial recrystallization is given successively, a uniform and refined grain structure is obtained. Once complete recrystallization takes place, successive recrystallization proceeds much more easily.
