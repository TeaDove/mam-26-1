## DEFORMATION IN NON-RECRYSTALLIZATION REGION

## Retardation of recrystallization

In order to decrease grain size further, deformation in the non-recrystallization region must be applied following deformation in the recrystallization region.

13 Recrystallization of austenite following single deformation of 50% in various steels; after Irvine et al.

In plain C steel, recrystallization becomes sluggish at low temperatures in the γ-region (about 900 °C ≈ Ar3 ). Nb (niobium) has a powerful influence in retarding γ-recrystallization (austenite recrystallization), thereby achieving a non-recrystallized state, see Figs. 13 and 14. They show a marked temperature dependence of the progress of recrystallization, and also a large recrystallization-retarding effect of Nb. Kozasu et al. reported that, besides a very large retarding effect of Nb, V had some retarding effect, while Al (aluminium) and Al+N accelerated recrystallization slightly. Although both V and Nb are strong carbide-forming elements the former does not have any retarding effect, while the latter exhibits a very strong retarding effect. This is considered due to differences in solubility in γ , and strain-induced precipitation potential between the two elements. The retardation of recrystallization due to Nb can be exhibited only when Nb is in solution in γ before deformation. If Nb remains undissolved because of a low heating temperature, it does not demonstrate any delaying effect.

The recrystallization retardation due to Nb has been discussed by many investigators. It has been attributed to (i) a solute-drag effect, (ii) strain-induced precipitation of fine Nb(C,N), or (iii) the combined effects of solute Nb and precipitation of fine Nb(C,N). Coladas et al. reported that the main influence of Nb alloying is on the incubation time for the start of recrystallization, and not on the kinetics of recrystallization after incubation and, further, that the delayed recrystallization is due to a solute effect, see Fig. 15.
