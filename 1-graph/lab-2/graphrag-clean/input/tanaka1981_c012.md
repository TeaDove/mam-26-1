The α-grain size correlates with effective γ interfacial area $S_{\mathrm{V}}$ (grain-boundary area plus deformation band), shown in Fig. 20. Although an increase in $S_{\mathrm{V}}$ causes α-grain (ferrite grain) refinement, for a fixed $S_{\mathrm{V}}$ value deformation in the non-recrystallization region refines α more effectively than that in the recrystallization region. According to Ouchi et al., in the early stage of transformation the transformation rate is given by $N_{S} \times S_{V}$, where $N_{S}$ is the nucleation rate per unit boundary area. While γ-grain refinement through recrystallization increases only the $S_{V}$ value, deformation in the non-recrystallization region increases both $N_{S}$ and $S_{V}$. The unrecrystallized γ-structure contains a high dislocation density and substructure besides elongated grain boundary and deformation band, which surely enhance the nucleation rate $N_{S}$. In a sense, general diffusional transformation controlled by the interface nucleation growth mechanism is accelerated by the deformation in the non-recrystallization region.

The ratio of γ-grain size to α-grain size accompanying transformation reduces with decreasing γ-grain size, reaching a limiting value of $\sim1$ in both hot-rolled and normalized steel. However, when rolled heavily in the non-recrystallization region, the ratio is 2.3–2.5, where the α-grain size is determined by the linear intercept method in the through-thickness direction, showing the superior effect on grain refinement of deformation in the non-recrystallization region. In practice, when finely recrystallized γ is given 70–80% reduction in the non-recrystallization region, the ASTM grain-size no. is $\sim12$ , whereas normalization produces, at most, grain size no. $\sim10$ .
