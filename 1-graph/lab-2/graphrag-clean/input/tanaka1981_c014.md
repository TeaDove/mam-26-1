## Formation of deformation band

Figure 18 shows an elongated γ-grain (austenite grain) structure produced by deformation in the γ-region (Fig. 18a) and partially transformed grain structure (Fig. 18b). α-grains (ferrite grains) are nucleated in deformation bands as well as γ-grain boundaries, individual grains being formed frequently within γ-grains. Although the nature of deformation bands is not known exactly, they generally exist in closely packed pairs of parallel lines which often terminate within a grain, creating a twin-like pattern in heterogeneous distribution. The studies on deformation bands are not numerous. As shown in Fig. 19, deformation-band density increases a little in the strain range of less than $30\%$, beyond which it increases rapidly with increasing reduction. The deformation-band density is barely affected by deformation temperature in the non-recrystallization region, but it decreases rapidly when the temperature exceeds ~1000 °C while according to Sekine and Maruyama the apparent γ-grain-boundary area (γ-grain boundary plus deformation bands) increases with an increasing amount of reduction, reaching a steady level above $\sim 30\%$ reduction. It is worth noting that, with an increasing amount of reduction, the γ-grain-boundary area increases gradually, while deformation-band density increases rapidly, indicating that α-grain refinement resulting from deformation in the non-recrystallization region is due mainly to an increase in the deformation band. The grain-boundary area is increased only by a factor of about 2 when total deformation in the non-recrystallization region is 70–80%. It is also important to note that even when the deformation band is saturated in number, about 10% of γ-grains contain very few deformation bands, and that not all deformation bands have the same ferrite-nucleation potential; some bands only have very thin, straight bands of α , implying poor nucleation capacity. Even the introduction of deformation bands cannot bring about uniform and homogeneous γ-grain structure and thereby homogeneous α-grain structure. Inhomogeneity in grain structure is the fate of as-hot-rolled material which can, however, be reduced with a decrease in recrystallized γ-grain size before deformation in the non-recrystallization region.
