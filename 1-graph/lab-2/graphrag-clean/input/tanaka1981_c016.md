## DEFORMATION IN AUSTENITE—FERRITE REGION

## Change in microstructure and mechanical properties

A limiting γ-grain (austenite grain) size achieved by deformation in the recrystallization region is broken by deformation in the non-recrystallization region. Grain refinement due to deformation in the non-recrystallization region reaches a limiting value at 60–70% reduction. This limit can be broken only by deformation in the γ–α (austenite–ferrite) two-phase region. Besides grain refinement, deformation in the two-phase region gives a substantially different effect on microstructure and mechanical properties. Microstructural change accompanying deformation in the two-phase region has already been shown schematically in Fig.4. Figure 21 shows the changes in intensity of the (200) reflection, strengths, and toughness with deformation in the two-phase region. With an increasing amount of deformation, the yield and tensile strengths increase, while the transition temperature and absorbed energy decrease. Microstructural change accompanying deformation in the two-phase region is shown in Fig.22. Deformation in the γ-region alone produces a microstructure consisting of equiaxed α-grains (ferrite grains) with low dislocation density (see Fig.22a and b). Deformation in the two-phase region produces a mixed grain structure, which consists of equiaxed α-grains and 'cold-worked' grains in terms of an optical microscopic scale, or consists of polygonal grains and subgrains in terms of an electron microscopic scale (see Fig. 22c and d). The deformed γ transforms to polygonal α, while the deformed α changes into subgrains. In deformed α, recrystallization is very sluggish, producing subgrains. This is because of the stabilization of the α sub-boundary network due to strain-induced precipitation of Nb(C, N) and/or V(C, N). It is postulated that, during rolling, the γ to α transformation can nucleate on the unrecovered dislocation substructure. Thus, formed α-grains soon impinge on existing γ-subgrains and cannot grow further. This kind of mutual interaction between deformed γ and α produces a further decrease in α-grain size.

Deformation in the two-phase region produces a duplex structure consisting of soft polygonal grains and hard subgrains. On the other hand, deformation of γ produces a single structure consisting of soft grains alone. This difference in microstructure between the two controlled-rolling conditions brings about a difference in mechanical properties between the two materials.
