The cause of through-thickness embrittlement has been studied by many investigators, and the following suggested: (i) a crack initiates at an MnS inclusion flattened by low-temperature finish rolling, and propagates along grain with $\{100\}$ orientation and/or a banded structure; (ii) the clusters of crystal grains with $\{100\}$ orientation are the main sources of separation cracks, since separation occurs even in steel with an extremely low S content, and this is strongly supported by the observation that separation increased with increasing intensity in the $\{100\}$ texture; (iii) intergranular failure along prior γ-boundary (austenite boundary), probably embrittled by the segregation of impurities, increases the occurrence of separation; (iv) in hot-rolled strip, temper embrittlement caused by the segregation of P to α-grain (ferrite grain) boundaries in the rich layer during slow cooling after coiling deteriorates boundary toughness; (v) dimensional anisotropy of the microstructure produced by controlled rolling — γ-grain in the case of the intergranular fracture, and α-grain in the case of transgranular — is the main cause of through-thickness embrittlement; (vi) pearlite and/or a bainite banded structure; and (vii) a combination of these effect.

The mechanism initiating separation may depend on chemical composition, microstructure, and hot-rolling condition. When stress is induced in the through-thickness direction, cracks initiate at the place where the fracture strength is lowest, and propagate along the second weakest location. When flattened MnS exists in steel, it triggers the initiation of the crack, which propagates along grain with $\{100\}$ orientation and/or bainite and/or the pearlite band, depending on the situation. If MnS exists very occasionally in steel with an extremely low S content, separation will initiate at the second weakest location.

Separation has a large effect on transition temperature and absorbed energy. The latter is influenced most markedly near the temperature where shear area becomes just 100%, exhibiting the greatest number of separation. As shown in Fig. 31, both absorbed energy and transition temperature decrease with an increasing number of separation. As is clear from the above explanation, the low-temperature toughness in the L and T direction is improved by the sacrifice of that in the Z direction.

## Elimination of separation

Various methods have been proposed to prevent the occurrence of separation. In order to eliminate flattened MnS which triggers the initiation of separation, inclusion-shape control has been employed, which converts elongated sulphide to a globular inclusion by the addition of rare earth metals and/or Ca. Advancing the sulphide-shape control technique, a new desulphurization method has been developed, which can reduce the S content to an extremely low level. Finish rolling in the γ-region results in the formation of a weak $\{100\}$ texture, suppressing markedly the occurrence of separation. A decrease in the P content is desirable, since it causes temper embrittlement during slow cooling after coiling in hot-rolled strip. Once initiated separation has a propensity to propagate along the banded structure. Mori et al. reported that the occurrence of separation is determined by the combined effect of band density and $\{100\}$ intensity, and that the addition of Cr disperses a banded structure, thereby suppressing the occurrence of separation.
