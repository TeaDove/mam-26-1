From a theoretical point of view, Heslop and Petch showed that the ductile-to-brittle transition temperature is inversely proportional to $d^{-1/2}$, where d is the grain size. Based on the observation that, in a martensitic structure, the facet unit was the effective grain size, Kunitake et al. gave the transition temperature as

$$
T _ {\mathbf {r s}} = A - B \log d _ {\mathrm{e}} ^ {- 1 / 2} \tag{12}
$$

where $A$ and $B$ are constant. Gladman et al. gave the transition temperature of bainitic steel by the equation:

$$
\begin{array}{r l} T _ {\mathrm{rs}} (^ {\circ} \mathrm{C}) & = - 19 + 44 (\% \mathrm{Si}) + 700 (\% \mathrm{N} _ {\mathrm{free}}) ^ {1 / 2} \\ & \quad + 0.26 (\sigma_ {\mathrm{D}} + \Delta P + \Delta) - 11.5 d ^ {- 1 / 2} \end{array}\tag{13}
$$

where $\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}$ is Ashby-Orowan strengthening from dispersed second-phase particles, and $\Delta P$ and $\Delta$ are the assumed strengthening contribution from random or forest dislocation and the contribution from dislocation in low-angle boundary, respectively. As is clear from equation (13), all of the strengthening factor raise the transition temperature. The only exception is grain refinement, which increases yield strength and at the same time reduces transition temperature.

The sub-boundary cannot be an obstacle to the propagation of brittle cracking. However, if sub-grain form a cluster, thereby developing a high-angle boundary, this can arrest brittle cracking. As shown in Fig. 31, the transition temperature decreases in a linear manner with the number of separation. Taking these effect into consideration, Tanaka proposed the following equation:

$$
\begin{array}{r l} T _ {\mathrm{rs}} = \Delta T + A (\sigma_ {\mathrm{ph}} + \sigma_ {\mathrm{dh}} + \Delta) & \\ - B d ^ {- 1 / 2} (1 - f _ {\mathrm{s}}) - C d _ {\mathrm{e}} ^ {- 1 / 2} f _ {\mathrm{s}} - E n _ {\mathrm{s}} \end{array}\tag{14}
$$

where $\Delta T$ is a constant depending on chemical composition; $A, B, C$, and $E$ are constant; $f_{\mathrm{S}}$ is the volume fraction of subgrain; $d_{\mathrm{e}}$ is the effective grain size, where $d_{\mathrm{e}}$ is in between the equiaxed grain and subgrain size; and $n_{\mathrm{S}}$ is the number of separation.
