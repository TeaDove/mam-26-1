As shown in Fig. 22, α-phase (ferrite phase) deformed in the two-phase region remains mainly in the recovered condition in Nb (niobium) steel. This means that the texture developed by deformation in the two-phase region is the 'cold-rolled' texture. One example of texture developed by deformation in the two-phase region is shown in Fig. 25. Exhibiting ferrite—pearlite structures, the three have basically the same texture, the intensity being raised with an increasing reduction in the two-phase region. A weak texture with $\{112\}\langle 110\rangle$ and $\{554\}\langle 225\rangle$ components develops by heavy deformation in the γ-region (austenite region) alone. Deformation in the two-phase region produced textures consisting of (i) $\langle 110\rangle \|RD$ partial fibre texture, (ii) $\langle 100\rangle \|ND$ texture (ND is normal direction, where the $\langle 100\rangle$ axis is perpendicular to the plate surface), (iii) $\langle 111\rangle \|ND$, and (iv) $\langle 110\rangle \|TD$ (transverse direction) texture with a strong $\{554\}$ $\langle 225\rangle$ component. Among those four textures, (iii) and (iv) do not have a detrimental effect on mechanical properties, but (i) causes plane anisotropy and (ii) causes embrittlement in the through-thickness direction. The plane anisotropy in mechanical properties caused by the $\langle 110\rangle \|RD$ texture was examined theoretically and experimentally by Inagaki et al., shown in Figs. 26 and 27, where toughness is represented in terms of normal stress acting on the cleavage surface, i.e. the $\{100\}$ plane. The diagonal $(45^{\circ})$ direction has low strength but a high susceptibility to cleavage fracture (high transition temperature). The longitudinal $(0^{\circ})$ and transverse $(90^{\circ})$ directions exhibit high strength and low susceptibility to cleavage fracture (low transition temperature). Figure 28 shows plane anisotropy developed in steels finish rolled in the non-recrystallization region or in the two-phase region. The former develops a weak texture, showing indistinct anisotropy, while the latter develops strong plane anisotropy due to an intensive texture, which is very similar to Figs. 26 and 27.
