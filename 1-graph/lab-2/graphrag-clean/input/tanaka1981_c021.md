## Separation

In controlled-rolled steel, a matter more serious than plane anisotropy is embrittlement in the through-thickness direction, resulting mainly from the formation of a $\{100\}$ texture. Figure 29 shows schematically the full Charpy curves and number of separations in the L(longitudinal) and Z(through-thickness) directions, where curves of the T(transverse) direction are located near those of the L direction. The difference in transition temperature between the L and Z directions is more than 100 K. The low transition temperature in the L direction is due to separations (very often called delamination or splitting) which occur in a brittle manner in a fractured Charpy specimen parallel to the plate surface. As shown in Fig. 30, separation is greatest near the temperature where the ductile-fracture area becomes just 100%, being nil in the brittle range or at high temperatures. In contrast to conventionally hot-rolled steel, the Charpy impact energy of controlled-rolled steel increases with increasing temperature even in the ductile-fraction region, while the number of separations decreases. Impact energy reaches the shelf energy and separation disappears when the temperature reaches the transition temperature in the Z direction.
