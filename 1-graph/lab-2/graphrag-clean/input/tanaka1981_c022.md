The occurrence of separations, accompanied by a lowering of the transition temperature, has been explained in terms of the critical stress criterion. When the Charpy specimen is given a bending force the maximum stresses are induced at the plastic/elastic interface, just beneath a notch under the plane-strain condition. Induced stress is greatest in the $L$ direction, followed by the $Z$ and $T$ directions, when a longitudinal specimen is considered. The condition for the occurrence of separation is given by

$$
\sigma_ {\mathrm{f}} ^ {\mathrm{L}} > \sigma_ {\mathrm{L}} \quad , \tag{5}
$$

$$
\sigma_ {\mathrm{f}} ^ {\mathrm{Z}} = \sigma_ {\mathrm{Z}} \quad , \tag{6}
$$

where $\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{L}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{Z}}$ are the cleavage fracture strengths in the $L$ and $Z$ directions, and $\sigma_{\mathrm{L}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{Z}}$ are the stresses acting in the $L$ and $Z$ directions, respectively. By subtracting (6) from (5), we obtain

$$
\sigma_ {\mathrm{f}} ^ {\mathrm{L}} - \sigma_ {\mathrm{f}} ^ {\mathrm{Z}} \geqslant \sigma_ {\mathrm{L}} - \sigma_ {\mathrm{Z}} = k ^ {\prime} \sigma_ {\mathrm{y}} \tag{7}
$$

where $\sigma_{y}$ is the uniaxial yield stress and $k'$ is a constant. As is clear from equation (7), separation occurs only when there is anisotropy in the fracture strength, i.e. only when fracture strength is lower for the Z direction than for the L direction. As the occurrence of separation changes the stress state from plane strain to plane stress, it becomes more difficult for brittle fracture to occur. Embury et al. and Miyoshi et al. reported that separated material behaves in a similar manner to that of laminated plate.
