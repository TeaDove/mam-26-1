## PRACTICE OF CONTROLLED ROLLING IN PRODUCTION MILL

In the production mill, factor governing the controlled-rolling process are inter-related and a variety of approaches can be used to attain the same objective. The important factor are as follows: (i) lowering of the slab-reheating temperature to obtain small and uniform γ-grain (austenite grain) size; (ii) γ-grain refinement via recrystallization; (iii) selection of a suitable pass schedule and, in particular, the amount of reduction per pass during the initial pass to obtain a fine and uniform recrystallized γ-grain structure; (iv) selection of temperature and time of delay between the recrystallization region and the non-recrystallization region; and (v) a suitable amount of reduction and rolling temperature in the two-phase region. They are virtually equivalent to and interchangeable with each other, implying that a suitable combination of some of them would suffice to control-roll steel adequately. It is for this reason that the stressing factor differs from one investigator to another and from one mill to another. Taking into consideration the capacity, efficiency, cooling power, and stability of operation, each mill has its own controlled-rolling process.

The plate mill has difficulty in giving a sufficient amount of reduction per pass and, in particular, during the initial several pass of slab rolling. An improper pass schedule leads to mixed grain structure, causing deterioration in toughness. In order to overcome this difficulty, some investigators emphasize extremely large deformation in the non-recrystallization region. On the other hand, owing to a great deal of freedom in selecting the rolling temperature and distribution of reduction among the recrystallization, non-recrystallization, and two-phase region, the plate mill can meet freely a variety of requirements. In the hot-strip mill, the reduction per pass can be more than the critical one required for the completion of recrystallization in each pass, which makes reduction very easy in the recrystallization region, producing a finer and more uniform γ-grain structure than the plate mill. In the strip mill, however, the time and temperature of holding, distribution of reduction among the recrystallization, non-recrystallization, and two-phase region, and selection of the finishing temperature are quite restricted. Thus, the plate mill and hot-strip mill have their advantages and disadvantages. The practice of controlled rolling is described briefly below.

As shown in Fig. 1, controlled rolling in the plate mill is often carried out in term of a 'rough—hold—finish' schedule: rolling passes in the rougher in the rapid-recrystallization region, a delay period, and rolling passes in the finisher. A delay is necessary to allow a specified total deformation to be introduced in the non-recrystallization region. There is a great possibility that during the holding period, recrystallization and subsequent rapid grain growth will take place or partial recrystallization will occur whereby a mixed α-grain (ferrite grain) structure is formed, deteriorating toughness. Care must be taken, therefore, to ensure the required low finishing temperature while preventing rapid grain growth or partial recrystallization during holding.

Instead of the rough—hold—finish schedule, Jones and Rothwell adopted a 'continuous' schedule involving reduction in the whole range, which produces a uniform, fine-grained structure. Figure 35 depicts the new rolling schedule, i.e. the three-stage rolling process as well as the standard two-stage process (rough—hold—finish), employed by USINOR, Dunkirk. During the delay time at high temperature in the standard two-stage process, recrystallization is rapid even in Nb (niobium) steel, leading to a coarse-grained structure at the end of the delay time. In the three-stage process, the first delay time at high temperature leads to a coarse-grained structure. During the second delay time, recrystallization is sluggish so that the grain size at the end of rolling is finer for the three-stage process than for the second stage.

At the British Steel Corporation, General Steels Division, to minimize recrystallization and grain growth during the delay period, the temperature at the commencement of the delay period is reduced by reducing the reheating temperature.
