The temperature at the end of the delay period is controlled in order to allow a specific total deformation to be introduced without inducing an excessively high mill loading. Finish rolling at temperature in the two-phase and/or α-region is employed to increase strength. Total rolling deformation in the non-recrystallization region after the delay period is very important in order to achieve a uniform, fine-grained structure. To fulfil this requirement, the thickness of material at the commencement of re-rolling after the delay period is controlled at either three time or five time that of the final thickness.

Controlled rolling in the hot-strip mill is very different from that in the plate mill: (i) rapid cooling on the run-out table after finish rolling is followed by slow cooling after coiling; (ii) both the rougher and finisher can carry out heavy reduction — during rolling in the finisher, the interpass time is very short, total rolling deformation in the finisher becoming almost one-pass reduction; (iii) rolling temperature and total reduction in the finisher encounter much restriction; (iv) rougher rolling can be regarded as deformation in the recrystallization region, and finisher rolling as deformation in the non-recrystallization region — the total reduction is overwhelmingly larger for rougher rolling than for finisher rolling and reduction per pass is large, which is suitable for achieving fine-grained γ (austenite) through repeated recrystallization, resulting in a uniform, fine-grained α-structure; and (v) during holding at the coiling temperature, precipitation of Nb and V proceeds causing a large increase in yield strength. Rolling in the two-phase region is not always carried out, causing few separation. Because of large precipitation hardening and few separation, the transition temperature is usually much higher for hot-rolled strip than for plate.
