## STRENGTHENING AND TOUGHENING MECHANISMS

## Equations describing strength and toughness

The strengthening mechanism in controlled-rolled steels will be described briefly. The yield stress of polycrystalline solids follows the Hall–Petch relation:

$$
\left| \begin{array}{c}\sigma_ {\mathbf {y}} = \sigma_ {\mathbf {o}} + k _ {\mathbf {y}} d ^ {- 1 / 2} \\ = (\sigma_ {\mathrm{lh}} + \sigma_ {\mathrm{sh}} + \sigma_ {\mathrm{dh}} + \sigma_ {\mathrm{ph}}) + k _ {\mathbf {y}} d ^ {- 1 / 2}. \end{array} \right|\tag{8}
$$

where $\sigma_{0}$ is internal stress, d is grain size, and $k_{y}$ is a constant; $\sigma_{0}$ is divided into several terms: lattice hardening, solid-solution hardening, dislocation hardening, and precipitation hardening. Observing extra hardening due to texture development, Little et al. and Coleman et al. proposed to add texture hardening $\sigma_{text}$ to equation (8) which was, however, found to be quite small. Baker and McPherson observed strengthening due mainly to subgrain hardening in microstructure mixed with subgrains:

$$
\sigma_ {\mathbf {y}} = \sigma_ {0} + k _ {\mathbf {y}} d ^ {- 1 / 2} + \sigma_ {\mathrm{text}} + \sigma_ {\mathrm{sub}} \tag{9}
$$

Matsuda et al. reported that in the martensitic structure the prior γ-grain (austenite grain) was divided into several blocks, which play the same role with large-angle grains, becoming obstacles to the propagation of yield and cleavage cracks. The concept of this 'effective grain size' was evidenced by other investigators. Equation (8) is rewritten as

$$
\sigma_ {\mathbf {y}} = \sigma_ {0} + k d _ {\mathrm{e}} ^ {- 1 / 2} \tag{10}
$$

where $d_{e}$ is the effective grain size. Deformation in the two-phase region produces a mixed grain structure consisting of polygonal grains and sub-grains or cellular substructures. Bramfitt and Marder proposed that the cell contribution to yield strength could be added by allowing for respective volume fractions of grains and cells in a partially recrystallized structure as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{r l} \sigma_ {\mathbf {y}} = \sigma_ {\mathbf {o}} + k _ {\mathbf {y}} d ^ {- 1 / 2} (1 - f _ {\mathbf {c}}) \\ + k _ {\mathbf {c}} d _ {\mathbf {c}} ^ {- 1 / 2} f _ {\mathbf {c}} \end{array}\tag{11}
$$

where $f_{\mathbf{c}}$ is the fraction of cell structure, and $k_{\mathbf{y}}$ and $k_{\mathbf{c}}$ are the experimentally determined values for plots of fully recrystallized structures and fully cellular structures, respectively.
