## Interpretation of mechanical properties

The reports on variation in transition temperature with deformation temperature in the two-phase region have been in disagreement with each other. As shown in Fig. 33, the transition temperature increased with a decrease in finishing temperature in the two-phase region; it reached a minimum at a certain temperature in the two-phase region, and increased again with a further decrease in finishing temperature. The transition temperature decreased with decreasing deformation temperature; excessive reduction in the two-phase region raised the transition temperature; and heavy reduction in the two-phase region lowered the transition temperature of plain carbon steel. Those contradictory reports are explained consistently by equation (14). In steel deformed in the two-phase region, the second term $A(\sigma_{\mathrm{ph}} + \sigma_{\mathrm{dh}} + \Delta)$ and the last term $En_{\mathrm{S}}$ on the right-hand side of equation (14) have a particularly large influence on transition temperature. If the contribution to the transition temperature from the second term, in particular dislocation hardening $\sigma_{\mathrm{dh}}$, is larger than that from separation, the transition temperature increases. On the other hand, if the contribution from separation is larger than that from dislocation hardening, the transition temperature decreases.

When deformed just below the Ar3 temperature, the cellular dislocation structure changes quickly into a subgrain structure, eliminating large dislocation hardening. However, when deformed at lower temperatures, insufficient recovery raises $\sigma_{\mathrm{dh}}$, which exceeds the effect of separation and thereby increases the transition temperature. In Nb (niobium) steel, conflict between dislocation hardening and separation is severe because fine precipitates of strain-induced Nb(C, N) pin down dislocations, and thereby retard recovery and recrystallization. On the other hand, in C-Mn steel, dislocation hardening is small owing to the rapid progress of recovery and, at the same time, there is a large effect of separation due to the development of $\{100\}$ texture, which causes a decrease in transition temperature even when finish rolled at low temperatures and/or heavily deformed.
