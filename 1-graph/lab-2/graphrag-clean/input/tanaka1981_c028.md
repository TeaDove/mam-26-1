## SUMMARY

A review has been made of the controlled rolling of steel, covering such aspects as its historical development, the fundamentals of controlled rolling, recrystallization behaviour in Nb (niobium) steel, the formation and role of deformation band, properties of controlled-rolled steel, effect of alloying elements, the practice of controlled rolling in production mill, and future development.

Rolling passes in the low-temperature range just above Ar3 were made to refine the grain structure and thereby to enhance both strength and toughness in as-rolled steel. Their influence was found to be very beneficial when applied to Nb steel. This was the beginning of controlled rolling.

Controlled rolling consists of three stage: (i) stage 1 is deformation in the recrystallization region, where coarse austenite is refined by recrystallization but still transforms to a relatively coarse ferrite; (ii) stage 2 is deformation in the non-recrystallization region, where deformation band are formed in unrecrystallized austenite—ferrite nucleates on the deformation bands as well as on the austenite grain boundary, giving fine ferrite; and (iii) stage 3 is deformation in the austenite—ferrite two-phase region — deformation in this region continues stage 2 and also deforms the ferrite, producing a substructure. Additional strengthening is obtained by the subgrain hardening.

The fundamental difference between conventionally hot-rolled and controlled-rolled steel lies in that in the former ferrite nucleates exclusively at austenite grain boundary, whereas in the latter ferrite nucleation occurs in the grain interiors as well as the grain boundary. This brings about a large difference in the final ferrite grain size between the two.

Controlled-rolled steel has higher strength and a lower transition temperature than conventionally hot-rolled or normalized steel. In controlled-rolled steel, however, plane anisotropy in the mechanical properties and through-thickness embrittlement are caused by crystallographic texture development, manganese sulphide inclusion flattened by low-temperature rolling, and others. Through-thickness embrittlement is the main cause of the occurrence of separation which appear in the fractured Charpy specimens in the longitudinal and transverse direction. Very low transition temperatures in the longitudinal and transverse direction are achieved by sacrificing toughness in the through-thickness direction. Several procedures are proposed to eliminate separation.

Recrystallization behaviour is divided into the recovery, partial recrystallization, and complete recrystallization region. To obtain uniform, fine-grained austenite and ferrite structure, at least one rolling pass must be performed successively in a strain range larger than that for the partial-recrystallization region. The retardation mechanism of austenite recrystallization due to Nb is explained in term of strain-induced precipitation of fine Nb(C, N) and/or solute-drag effect. Deformation band generated by controlled rolling divide an austenite grain into several blocks, since they are equivalent to austenite grain boundaries with regard to ferrite-nucleation potential. In controlled-rolled steel, the microstructure consists of fine-grained polygonal ferrite or a mixture of polygonal ferrite grain and subgrain, depending on whether deformation is finished in the austenite or austenite—ferrite region. Taking microstructural change into consideration, strengthening and toughening mechanism are presented. Transition temperature is determined mainly by the following two contradictory factor; hardening due to precipitation, dislocation, etc., and grain refinement and separation.

Although there are many processing variables governing controlled rolling, some of them are virtually equivalent to and interchangeable with each other. It is for this reason that the stressing factor is different from one mill to another. As controlled rolling is known to be very useful, it has been applied to various fields and its application is being expanded. Acicular ferrite and ferrite plus martensite dual-phase steel are produced by the combination of suitable chemical composition and controlled rolling. By making use of the grain-refining effect, controlled rolling is employed as pretreatment before normalizing or quenching.
