## EFFECT OF ALLOYING ELEMENTS

Alloying elements which enhance strength and toughness are most desirable. There are three ways of increasing strength; grain refining, precipitation and/or solid-solution hardening, and transformation hardening. An improvement in toughness is achieved by either grain refining or by toughening the α-matrix (ferrite matrix) itself. Since grain refining enhances both strength and toughness, grain-refining elements are most desirable. Grain refinement is attained by fine dispersion of a second phase and/or by lowering the transformation temperature by the addition of substitutional alloying elements. Needless to say the purpose of controlled rolling is to achieve grain refinement and thereby to improve mechanical properties. The effect of each alloying element is represented in terms of its contribution to strength and transition temperature. Besides strength and toughness, weldability is a very important factor, which improves with the decreasing carbon equivalent and, in particular, C content.

The microalloying elements, such as Nb (niobium) and Ti (titanium), are indispensable in controlled-rolled steel. In the early stage of development, Nb was employed mainly for precipitation hardening. However, since Nb was found to improve strength and toughness very effectively through grain refinement, it has become an indispensable element in controlled-rolled steel. The effects of Nb, V, and Ti on strength are shown in Fig. 34. Nb contributes to strength via both grain refining and precipitation; particularly in a low Nb content, major hardening is due to grain refinement. V causes a large increase in both strength and transition temperature through precipitation hardening. Ti causes a slight increase in strength via grain refinement and precipitation hardening. Therefore, only Nb enhances both strength and toughness in controlled-rolled steel. The main cause of grain refinement in Nb steel is that Nb in solution delays γ-recrystallization (austenite recrystallization), thereby raising the recrystallization temperature, extending the non-recrystallization region, and intensifying the effect of controlled rolling. It is also considered that strain-induced precipitates of fine Nb(C,N) prevent α-grain growth during the γ to α transformation.

In comparison with Nb, the effect of V on grain refinement is slight. The retarding effect of recrystallization was not observed at low temperatures in the γ-region. The addition of Ti slows down grain growth during slab reheating or after the recrystallization of γ. When added in large quantities ($\sim$0.1%), Ti was reported to retard the recrystallization of γ. However, since Ti decreases the toughness of both base steel and the heat-affected-zone of weldment, its use is limited. In general, precipitates formed in γ at high temperatures are incoherent, causing little increase in strength. While precipitates formed in the ferrite matrix are coherent and raise the strength and transition temperature rapidly. Hashimoto et al. showed that strain-induced precipitates raise strength, without impairing toughness, because of being incoherent but very fine. Controlled-rolled steel frequently makes combined use of Nb and V; Nb aids grain refining and V precipitation hardening.
