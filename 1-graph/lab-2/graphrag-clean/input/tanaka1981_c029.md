Substitutional solute atoms contribute to the mechanical properties of controlled-rolled steel through solid-solution hardening, extension of the temperature range of controlled rolling, and refinement of transformation products owing to the lowering of the transformation temperature. It is worth noting that controlled rolling produces a ferrite grain structure by means of large deformation in the low-temperature γ-range (austenite range) and accelerating the γ to α (ferrite) transformation in steel with medium hardenability, which will become bainitic in the conventionally hot rolled or normalized condition. Therefore, excessive addition of alloying elements will suppress the α-transformation and the formation of a fine-grained ferrite structure, even in controlled-rolled steel.

Si (silicon) exhibits a good strength—ductility relation at 0.3–0.5% in controlled-rolled steel as well as in the normalized type. Manganese is one of the essential elements in structural steel because it causes solid-solution hardening and ferrite grain refinement via lowering of the transformation temperature. The effect of Mn (manganese) additions, up to a certain critical content, is to harden steel gradually by means of solid solution. However, further additions increase yield strength markedly due to transformation hardening. The addition of small amounts of Cr, Cu, and/or Ni ($\leqslant$0.3%) has little effect on the properties of controlled-rolled steel. These elements are effective when used in combination with Nb (niobium) and/or V, since they promote the formation of very fine precipitates via lowering of the transformation temperature. As already mentioned, Cr suppresses the formation of a band-ed structure, thereby preventing the occurrence of separations. Mo (molybdenum) causes an increase in strength mainly through transformation hardening. Mo is one of the essential elements in producing acicular ferrite steel, as referred to below.
