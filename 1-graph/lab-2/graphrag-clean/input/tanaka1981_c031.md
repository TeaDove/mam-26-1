Instead of the rough—hold—finish schedule, Jones and Rothwell adopted a 'continuous' schedule involving reduction in the whole range, which produces a uniform, fine-grained structure. Figure 35 depicts the new rolling schedule, i.e. the three-stage rolling process as well as the standard two-stage process (rough—hold—finish), employed by USINOR, Dunkirk. During the delay time at high temperature in the standard two-stage process, recrystallization is rapid even in Nb (niobium) steel, leading to a coarse-grained structure at the end of the delay time. In the three-stage process, the first delay time at high temperature leads to a coarse-grained structure. During the second delay time, recrystallization is sluggish so that the grain size at the end of rolling is finer for the three-stage process than for the second stage.

At the British Steel Corporation, General Steels Division, to minimize recrystallization and grain growth during the delay period, the temperature at the commencement of the delay period is reduced by reducing the reheating temperature.

The temperature at the end of the delay period is controlled in order to allow a specific total deformation to be introduced without inducing an excessively high mill loading. Finish rolling at temperatures in the two-phase and/or α-regions (ferrite regions) is employed to increase strength. Total rolling deformation in the non-recrystallization region after the delay period is very important in order to achieve a uniform, fine-grained structure. To fulfil this requirement, the thickness of material at the commencement of re-rolling after the delay period is controlled at either three times or five times that of the final thickness.

Controlled rolling in the hot-strip mill is very different from that in the plate mill: (i) rapid cooling on the run-out table after finish rolling is followed by slow cooling after coiling; (ii) both the rougher and finisher can carry out heavy reduction — during rolling in the finisher, the interpass time is very short, total rolling deformation in the finisher becoming almost one-pass reduction; (iii) rolling temperature and total reduction in the finisher encounter much restriction; (iv) rougher rolling can be regarded as deformation in the recrystallization region, and finisher rolling as deformation in the non-recrystallization region — the total reduction is overwhelmingly larger for rougher rolling than for finisher rolling and reduction per pass is large, which is suitable for achieving fine-grained γ (austenite) through repeated recrystallization, resulting in a uniform, fine-grained α-structure; and (v) during holding at the coiling temperature, precipitation of Nb and V proceeds causing a large increase in yield strength. Rolling in the two-phase region is not always carried out, causing few separations. Because of large precipitation hardening and few separations, the transition temperature is usually much higher for hot-rolled strip than for plate.
