## As-hot-rolled dual-phase steel

Dual-phase steel has a microstructure consisting of α-grains (ferrite grains) dispersed with fine martensite islands. It is characterized by continuous yielding followed by rapid work hardening, which results in a low yield but high tensile strength and large elongation. Dual-phase steel can be produced in the as-hot-rolled condition by the combination of suitable chemical composition and rolling conditions. When steel containing both strong ferrite formers like Si (silicon) and transformation-delaying elements such as Cr, Mn (manganese), and/or Mo (molybdenum) is cooled after hot rolling, the microstructure consists of α - and γ-phases (austenite phases). The latter is stabilized due to carbon enrichment and thereby transforms to martensite at low temperatures. When deformation is applied in the non-recrystallization region, strain-assisted γ to α transformation occurs, thereby raising the α-transformation temperature on one hand and reducing the martensite transformation temperature on the other, which makes it easy to produce ferrite plus martensite dual-phase steel.

## Controlled rolling of C-Mn steel

Gohda et al. and Hashimoto et al. reported that when deformed in the two-phase region, Nb-free (niobium free) steel exhibits improvements in strength and toughness and, further, that heavy deformation in the two-phase region does not deteriorate toughness. Increase in strength by deformation in the two-phase region is due to either subgrain formation in the recovery region or dislocation hardening in the non-recovery region. The ease of recovery and occurrence of separations are responsible for good toughness of steel obtained by heavy deformation in the two-phase region. The effect of deformation in the two-phase region is essentially the same between Nb-bearing and Nb-free steels. The difference lies in the ease of recovery, which causes differences in the optimum conditions of finishing temperature and/or strain. Controlled-rolled C—Mn steel has started to be used as material for low-temperature service.
